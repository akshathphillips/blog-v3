# Craft guide: letters to Avi (`section: avi`)

Letters from a father to his daughter, for her to read when she's older. They
live on their own shelf at `/writing/avi/` and stay off the home page.

Reference posts: *Why I write to you* (the opening letter), *Love you, papa*.

**This is the most important writing on the site and the easiest to get wrong.**
One fabricated detail here is worse than a hundred missed posts.

## Form

- **Addressed to her**, second person. "You were supposed to be falling asleep."
- **Signed.** The opening letter signs `Dad`; memory letters sign `Papa`, which
  is what she calls him. Use **`Papa`** on its own final line unless he says
  otherwise. No leading dash: em dashes are banned site-wide (see "House style"
  in `context.md`), and a leading `- ` would render as a bullet.
- **Short.** 150–350 words. *Love you, papa* is ~280 and complete. A letter that
  runs long is usually explaining instead of remembering.
- **Present tense for the scene, past for the memory**; follow the reference
  posts rather than a rule; read them before writing one.
- No headings. It's a letter.

## The three things that make these work

**1. Concrete detail over feeling.** Don't write "it was a special moment."
Write the twin mattress, the milk, the great green room, the exact words she
said. The feeling arrives on its own, carried by the specifics. If a sentence
states an emotion, try deleting it; the letter almost always gets better.

**2. Restraint: name the smallness.** The move that made the Richmond letter
land was undercutting it: *"I don't think you'll remember this afternoon. You're
too small, and it was too ordinary: a nap, a book, a borrowed mattress."*
Insisting a moment was momentous makes it smaller. Admitting it was ordinary,
and writing it down anyway, is the entire point of the shelf.

**3. No lesson.** Don't draw a moral, don't zoom out to how fast time goes,
don't address future-Avi with advice. Let the moment be the moment. The closest
these get to a lesson is the reason the shelf exists, stated once in the opening
letter; it doesn't need restating.

## Facts are sacred

- **Quote her exactly.** If he writes that she said "love you papa," that is the
  line. Don't smooth it into "I love you, Papa" or make it more articulate than
  a two-year-old.
- **Family terms as he gives them.** *maasi* (aunt), *mamu* (uncle); use them
  untranslated and unexplained; this letter is for family. Get the relationship
  right or ask. He corrected "someone else's house" to "Nitin mamu's home".
  Place and people matter to him in these.
- **Invent nothing.** Not what she was wearing, not the weather, not a second
  thing she said. Texture in the *prose* is yours ("like you'd been saving them
  up"); events, quotes, names, and places are his.
- **Don't add a sibling, a milestone, or a developmental claim** that wasn't in
  what he sent.

## Privacy

The repo and site are public and this is a real child. The published letters
name her, one wedding, and two relatives; treat that as the ceiling. If a
memory he sends contains something more private (health, an address, someone
else's business, anything embarrassing to her later), write around it or ask.
The test: *would she be glad this is public at sixteen?*

## Mechanics

```yaml
---
title: Love you, papa
date: 2026-07-09
section: avi
description: A twin mattress in Richmond, Goodnight Moon, and the first time you said it on your own.
tags: avi, fatherhood, letters
---
```

- **Title**: her words when she gave you any; otherwise the plainest possible
  description of the moment.
- **Description**: the concrete objects of the scene, not the emotion.
- **Tags**: `avi, fatherhood, letters` as the base set, plus specifics.
- **Date**: the day it happened, not the day it's written.
